How the prices are made

Every price is a chance of winning, worked out by playing the rest of the competition out thousands of times. This page shows the real formula, and the numbers on it are read live from the running market.

What the market can see: only each team's uptime score from the scoring site, plus how many hosts red team holds on it (from PWNBoard). It cannot see injects or any other scoring, so a catch-all “unknown points” term (step 6) stands in for them. The settings below were picked by hand to make the odds lively, not fitted to past competitions. It is play money.
Waiting for the first round of scores. The formulas below fill in once they arrive.

The model, step by step

1Whole-game uptime rate

The share of the available uptime points a team has actually earned. “Scored rounds” counts only rounds that awarded points, so a paused scoring engine (lunch) does not count against anyone.

whole_game = score ÷ (scored_rounds × checks_per_round)
           = score ÷ (… × …)

2Recent form

The same rate over recent rounds only, as an exponentially weighted average, so a team that just started failing checks loses ground quickly. Old rounds count half as much every … rounds. A round where nobody scored is skipped.

α    = 1 − 0.5 ^ (1 ÷ …) = …
form ← form + α × (points_last_round ÷ checks_per_round − form)

2bBlend

The two rates are mixed. A higher form weight makes the odds react faster to recent rounds.

blended = (1 − w) × whole_game + w × form        w = …
          kept between … and 100%

3Red team access

Each host red team holds (it called back to PWNBoard in the last few minutes) takes a slice off the team's projected uptime, up to a cap.

penalty        = min(…, … × hosts_held)
projected_rate = blended × (1 − penalty)

4Projected final score

What the team has now, plus what it is expected to earn in the rounds left. The rounds left come from the competition end time and the measured length of a round.

points_left = rounds_left × checks_per_round = … × … = …
expected_final = score + points_left × projected_rate

5Luck we can model

Even at a fixed rate, checks pass or fail at random, and the rate itself is only roughly known.

play_sd = √( points_left × rate × (1 − rate) )
rate_sd = points_left × √( rate × (1 − rate) ÷ (checks_per_round × max(1, 2 × …)) )

6Everything we cannot see

Injects, adjustments and any other scoring are invisible to the market, so a large extra spread stands in for them. It shrinks as fewer rounds remain than the reference number, so odds settle toward the uptime leader near the end. This is the main reason a leader is never a sure thing, and the biggest lever on how lively the odds are.

unknown_sd = … × min(1, √(rounds_left ÷ …)) = … points now
total_sd   = √( play_sd² + rate_sd² + unknown_sd² )

7Simulate

The rest of the competition is played out … times. In each run, every team's final score is its expected final plus a random amount scaled by its total spread. The randomness is fat-tailed (a Student-t distribution with … degrees of freedom), so upsets are more likely than with a bell curve. The same random draws are reused every tick, so prices only move when the inputs change.

final = expected_final + total_sd × t_draw
win chance  = share of runs where the team has the highest final score
top 3 chance = share of runs where it finishes in the top three

8From chance to price

Chances are floored so nobody trades at 0%, renormalised to add up to 100%, then blended with the last tick's price so lines move like a market instead of jittering.

floored  = max(win_chance, …), then scaled to sum to 100%
price    = … × last_price + (1 − …) × floored
Yes price = price in cents      No price = 100 − Yes price
